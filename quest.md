## OUD Diagnosis

| Check | Diagnostic Criteria                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
|       | Opioids are often taken in larger amounts or over a longer period of time than intended.                                                                                                                                                   |
|       | There is a persistent desire or unsuccessful efforts to cut down or control opioid use.                                                                                                                                                    |
|       | A great deal of time is spent in activities necessary to obtain the opioid, use the opioid, or recover from its effects.                                                                                                                   |
|       | Craving, or a strong desire to use opioids. Recurrent opioid use resulting in failure to fulfill major role obligations at work, school or home.                                                                                           |
|       | Continued opioid use despite having persistent or recurrent social or interpersonal problems caused or exacerbated by the effects of opioids.                                                                                              |
|       | Important social, occupational or recreational activities are given up or reduced because of opioid use.                                                                                                                                   |
|       | Recurrent opioid use in situations in which it is physically hazardous Continued use despite knowledge of having a persistent or recurrent physical or psychological problem that is likely to have been caused or exacerbated by opioids. |
|       | Tolerance, as defined by either of the following: (a) a need for markedly increased amounts of opioids to achieve intoxication or desired effect (b) markedly diminished effect with continued use of the same amount of an opioid         |
|       | Withdrawal, as manifested by either of the following: (a) the characteristic opioid withdrawal syndrome (b) the same (or a closely related) substance are taken to relieve or avoid withdrawal symptoms                                    |

##### Severity: Mild: 2-3 symptoms. Moderate: 4-5 symptoms. Severe: 6 or more symptoms

## <font color="firebrick"> Oral Oxycodone Self-administration in Rats </font>

- Oxycodone is primarily prescribed as oral tablets

## Does Genetics Influence a Trait?

- Within-strain: genetically identical individuals (VE = environmental variance)
- Between-strain: genetic differences (VA = additive genetic variance)
- h² = VA / (VA + VE) directly estimable
- Replicable across labs, paradigms, and time

## Oral Self-administration Captures Key Features of OUD

<img src="./images/quest/oxyren_alldata_sexdiff.png">

<span id="cite">NIDA U01DA0536721, Sharp, Chen, Williams</span>

Note:

- Robust voluntary drug consumption without prior exposure
- Strong preference for active over inactive spout
- Large individual variation in intake escalation
- Cue-induced reinstatement (models relapse)
- Hypoalgesia after consumption / hyperalgesia during withdrawal
- Strong sex differences mirroring human patterns

- Females consume significantly more across all doses and session lengths
- Females: broader reinforcing dose range
- Females: more vigorous reinstatement of drug seeking
- Males: 25-100 fold increase varies dramatically by strain
- Similar pattern to human sex differences in opioid use

## Temporal Pattern of Augmented Intake

- Hour-by-hour analysis of 16-h sessions
- Augmenters: rapid "loading-up" during first 6 hours, steep decline by 7-11h
- Non-Augmenters: peak at hour 1, gradual decline
- By 13-16h, both groups converge
- Suggests difference in reward efficacy, not metabolism

---

## No Genetic Evidence that Oxycodone Metabolism is Involved

- Analyzed 1,263 SNPs in Cyp3a2, Cyp3a9, Cyp2d1
- No significant allele frequency differences between Augmenters and Non-Augmenters
- Pharmacokinetic differences may not contribute to the phenotype
- Points toward CNS reward mechanisms

## Hypotheses

- The Mechanism: Chronic opioid exposure induces differential molecular responses that are genetically determined via gene-environment (G×E) interactions.

- The Manifestation: A subset of these genetically encoded molecular adaptations drives neuroplastic changes that facilitate subsequent escalation of opioid intake.

- The Application: These distinct molecular signatures can serve as predictive biomarkers to identify individuals at high risk for developing OUD during prescription opioid therapy.

## <font color="firebrick"> Liking vs. Wanting </font>

![Figure 1](./images/quest/rat_licking_oxy.svg)

- Incentive sensitization theory (Robinson & Berridge)
- Hedonic "liking" = pleasure upon consumption
- Motivational "wanting" = incentive drive to seek drug
- Classical prediction: they uncouple during addiction progression
- Can we measure how much rats like and want using licking behavior?

## The Gene Prioritization Pipeline

- High-impact sequence variants (missense, nonsense, splice site, SVs from pangenome)
  - 18,646 variants, affecting 6,667 genes, including 3,930 protein-coding genes (de Jong, et al, Cell Genomics 2024)
- Cis-eQTL, cis-pQTL
  - Genenetwork.org, Phenogen.org
  - Brain pQTL (Li, et al. iScience, 2025)
- 3D chromatin for enhancer promoter interaction.
  - Hi-C (Kim, Sci Data, 2025, BioRxiv 2026)
- AI-driven neurobiological relation search
  - GeneCup (Gunturken, et al. S3, 2022)
- Cell-type expression concordance (mouse, rat, human)
- DNA methylation changes near candidate genes
- Concordance with human OUD GWAS hits

## The Chr 1 Locus and Vitamin D

<a href="https://genenetwork.org/search?species=rat&group=HXBBXH&type=Brain+mRNA&dataset=PGenRatBrn_rna_0520&search_terms_or=cyp2r1%0D%0A&search_terms_and=&accession_id=None&FormID=searchResult" target=_new>cis-eQTL for Cyp2r1</a> |

<a href="https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/genes/CYP2R1"> Vitamin D </a>

<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8195487/" target=_new> Vitamin D and opioid addiction</a>

<div style="display: flex; align-items: center;">
<div style="flex: 1;">

![intake by genotype](./images/quest/oxycodone_intake_by_genotype_square.png)

</div>
<div style="flex: 1;">

![neurovascular](./images/quest/neurovascular_unit_vitamin_d.jpg)

</div>

Note:

- Genome-wide significant for oxycodone intake in females
- Cyp2r1: 25-hydroxylates Vitamin D3 in brain pericytes
- Produces precursor (25-OH D3) for neurosteroid 1,25-OH2 D3
- Oxycodone intake was 3-fold greater in rat strains with Alt SNP compared to wild type (0/0). Heterozygous (0/1) were significantly less than homozygous strains. p<5x10-7 for genotype effect

The Vitamin D - Opioid Sensitivity Connection

- 1,25-OH2 D3 is a neurosteroid synthesized within neurons (Cyp27b1)
- Regulates NGF/GDNF synthesis, Ca²⁺ flux, neuroprotection
- Reduced Cyp2r1 → deficient 25-OH D3 → impaired neurosteroid synthesis
- Creates "opioid sensitivity syndrome"
- NOT about systemic vitamin D deficiency — it's intraneuronal

The evidence supporting CACNA1C as a plausible causal gene for socially acquired nicotine intravenous self-administration is **substantial**.
